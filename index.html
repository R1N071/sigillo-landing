From 20 January 2027, every machine placed on the EU market must collect evidence of interventions in its safety software, and keep a five-year tracing log it can hand to a national authority on a reasoned request.

Annex III 1.1.9 and 1.2.1(f), Regulation (EU) 2023/1230

Two clauses change what a robot must prove.

Annex III · 1.1.9

Intervention evidence

The machine must identify its safety-critical software at all times, in an easily accessible form, and collect evidence of legitimate or illegitimate interventions and configuration changes.

Annex III · 1.2.1(f)

Five-year tracing log

A tracing log of interventions and of safety-software versions, enabled for five years after upload, producible on a reasoned request from a competent national authority.

prEN 50742, the candidate harmonised standard for 1.1.9, is still a draft. There is no presumption of conformity yet. The Commission's Expert Group endorsed guide chapters on these clauses on 5 November 2026.

Five stages from event to evidence.

Stage 1

Watch everything that matters

An edge agent subscribes to ROS 2 topics and MCAP files, VDA 5050 and ISO 21423 fleet messages, and safety-controller events over OPC UA. Software and configuration inventory, interventions, protective stops, mission events.

Stage 2

Chain every event

Each event enters a SHA-256 hash chain in deterministic CBOR. Edit one row and every hash after it breaks. The chain is the spine of every bundle we export.

Stage 3

Sign the roots in hardware

Merkle roots are signed with a non-exportable ECDSA key inside a TPM 2.0 secure element, and sealed with qualified RFC 3161 timestamps. The log carries its own proof of time.

Stage 4

Keep it uneditable for five years

Store-and-forward at the edge, then WORM object storage with five-year retention per software version. No one can quietly rewrite last month.

Stage 5

Hand over proof, not promises

One click exports an evidence bundle: signed events, Merkle proofs, an SBOM, and a clause map to 1.1.9 and 1.2.1(f). Traces are checked against formal task models, below. An open-source verifier lets a notified body check it offline, without trusting us.

What should have happened, formalised.

Missions are modelled as Coloured Petri Nets, the same formalism used in safety research and our own tooling. Guards like speed ≤ zone limit while human present become checkable facts. When a run skips a required step, the deviation is flagged and pinned to the signed event that proves it.
